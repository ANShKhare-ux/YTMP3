Frequently asked questions.

Why choose local software over web-based online converters?
Cloud converter websites throttle download speeds to a single thread, introduce security risks via ad redirects, and frequently compress audio down to 128 kbps. SonicWave executes locally on your Windows PC with a 16-stream parallel turbo engine, local FFmpeg & yt-dlp extraction, zero advertisements, and authentic 320 kbps MP3 and 1080p MP4 output.
Do I need to install Python, FFmpeg, or modify system environment variables?
No. SonicWave is self-bootstrapping. When you launch start.bat on Windows, the software automatically downloads and verifies the official yt-dlp and FFmpeg binaries into its local server directory. No manual configuration is required.
Which audio and video formats are supported?
SonicWave supports MP3 (at 320k, 256k, 192k, and 128k CBR/VBR), AAC (M4A), uncompressed WAV, and FLAC for audio. For video, it supports MP4 in 1080p Full HD, 720p HD, and 480p SD with automatically multiplexed stereo audio.
Is SonicWave open-source and free of telemetry?
Yes. SonicWave is completely free and open-source. There are no subscriptions, no locked tiers, and zero user telemetry or URL tracking. All conversions happen entirely on your machine.
Does SonicWave inject album artwork and ID3 metadata tags?
Yes. Converted audio tracks automatically have ID3 tags embedded with the video title, uploader name, and high-resolution thumbnail artwork so your files display cleanly in Windows Media Player, iTunes, Groove, Spotify, and mobile players.
How are extraction rules updated when upstream video platforms change?
SonicWave includes a 1-click binary updater. Press Ctrl+K in the app and select 'Update Engine (yt-dlp)' to pull the latest extraction definitions directly from the official upstream repository in seconds.
